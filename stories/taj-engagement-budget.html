₹4 Lakh. A ₹7.5 Lakh Brief.

A story from the field, told by the Miracle Eventz team led by founder Deepak H. Sonavane.

A competitor had quoted ₹7.5 lakh for the engagement. The budget on the table was ₹4 lakh, and the brief hadn't shrunk to match — an evening haldi with playing colours, a morning kumkum function, all at the Taj.

A site visit changed the plan more than any spreadsheet could. The existing trees and green lighting on the property could do visual work a bigger budget elsewhere would have had to pay for. An entry platform went up between two trees. Games on one side, gift carts on the other, a large lit truss built around the colour requirement.

Two things had quietly gone missing along the way: a thousand gas balloons, cut for budget, and — discovered the night before the event — the plain-cloth wall the brief actually called for, once the white body-cloth option on hand was rejected as wrong for the space.

The balloons that had been cut for cost ended up decorating the gift carts an hour before the event even started.

A former team member sourced the right cloth between roughly 11 PM and 2 AM the night before. Fifty kilograms of coloured powder went into the haldi — so much that the leftover packets were thrown into the crowd rather than go unused.

The only change the family asked for, once the event was underway, was more flowers at the entrance. Afterward, one family member told Deepak, \u201cThank you for being my brother,\u201d and said they'd chosen the right person. They asked for help with another family event soon after, and referrals followed.

What it taught us

A smaller number doesn't have to mean a smaller experience. It usually just means the thinking has to work harder — using what's already on the site, and solving two problems with one move instead of cutting the moment altogether.
